# Marketing strategy for bandPromo operators

This document is for **artists and site operators** using bandPromo on their own domain. It explains a practical way to promote music when large platforms are built to keep people *inside* their apps — and how bandPromo is designed to help you send listeners **to your site**, not to compete for attention inside someone else's feed.

For product milestones and feature timing, see [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). For what is shipped today, see [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md).

---

## The problem (honestly)

Today's social and streaming platforms are strong magnets. They pull content in from everywhere, show a slice of it natively, and optimize for **time on their platform** — not for your full album, your story, or your direct income.

That creates a mismatch for many artists:

- Platforms reward short clips, reels, and constant posting.
- Artists often want listeners to experience **full tracks**, **context**, **lyrics**, **early access**, and **direct support**.
- Paid ads on those same platforms can work, but they are expensive, opaque, and still keep the relationship on the platform's terms.

bandPromo does not fix discovery by itself. It does not replace Spotify, YouTube, Instagram, or TikTok. It gives you a **home base you control** — and a strategy for using big platforms as **routers**, not as your only stage.

---

## Core principle: platform as router, not home

```text
Teaser (on social)  →  Bridge (one link)  →  Experience (your bandPromo site)
```

| Layer | Where it lives | Job |
|-------|----------------|-----|
| **Teaser** | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook, X | Stop the scroll; promise something more |
| **Bridge** | Bio link, pinned comment, story link, QR code, email | One clear path off the platform |
| **Experience** | Your bandPromo install on **your domain** | Full playback, pages, access tiers, support, depth |

You are not trying to beat the algorithm at its own game. You are making the **exit** feel like an upgrade: "The full song, the story, the early access — **here**."

## What bandPromo is for in marketing terms

bandPromo helps you:

- **Own the presentation** — pages, player, visuals, and copy on your domain
- **Own the listening experience** — playlists and lyrics in your player (not a buried streaming link)
- **Own the relationship** — signups, VIP access, analytics on *your* site (as features mature)
- **Repeat work once** — build the release page and campaign assets from the same content (more automation in later versions)

bandPromo does **not**:

- Guarantee a new audience out of thin air
- Replace paid promotion entirely (but reduces dependence on it)
- Host your career inside Facebook or Google's walled garden
- Act as a central music discovery catalogue for all bandPromo sites

**Realistic pitch to yourself:** bandPromo makes each person who already cares about you **more valuable** — longer listens, return visits, signups, support — instead of sending them to a generic link tree or a file dump.

## Low-cost tactics that fit this model

These work with self-hosted sites and do not require large ad budgets.

### 1. One home link everywhere

Use **one primary URL** (your domain or a stable subdomain) in:

- Instagram / TikTok / YouTube bio
- Link fields on Spotify, Bandcamp, Discogs, etc.
- Email signature and newsletter footers

Avoid splitting attention across ten different tools. Your bandPromo site is the hub; other services are spokes you link *from* your pages when needed.

### 2. Posts that deliberately send people away

Social posts work better as **invitations**, not as the full product:

- "Full track + lyrics + the story behind the song → link in bio"
- "VIP heard this on Tuesday — everyone else Friday → our site"
- "B-side and live version only on our player"

Scarcity and completeness live on **your** site. The platform only gets the hook.

### 3. Give reasons the platform cannot match

Use bandPromo for what feeds and native players do poorly:

- Full album or playlist flow with lyrics
- Rich release pages (bio, news, tour, visuals)
- Private or VIP listening (as access tiers ship)
- Direct support links without platform skim
- Credible press/EPK-style pages for journalists and curators

### 4. Physical world bridges (often underrated)

QR codes on posters, setlists, merch, vinyl inserts, and gig flyers point to your site. Offline intent is often higher than cold feed traffic — and costs no ad spend.

### 5. Communities that still allow outbound links

Newsletters, mailing lists, Discord, fan forums, local press, podcasts, and radio show notes still send people **out** to URLs you control. They are less glamorous than viral reels but durable.

### 6. Press and curator pages

Bloggers, playlist curators, and local media want **one professional page** — not a 30-second reel. A clear release page on your domain beats another clip in the feed.

### 7. Capture people who already clicked once

Before spending on ads, improve what happens **after** the first visit:

- Email or newsletter signup (when you add it)
- Clear "new release" or news pages
- Return-visitor analytics on your install
- Simple reasons to come back (early access, credits, new tracks — as those features ship)

## What to avoid

- **Volume war on Reels** against labels with content teams
- **Buying reach to cold audiences** before your destination page is compelling
- **Hosting the full experience inside social embeds** — you lose the relationship and the data
- **Expecting bandPromo to be Spotify with discovery** — that is a different product and a fight on platform terms
- **Link trees with twelve equal destinations** — weak bridge layer; one hub, then depth

## A simple campaign workflow (today + future)

1. **Build the experience first** on bandPromo: release page, player, visuals, FAQ/login copy.
2. **Choose one bridge URL** for the campaign (release page or player entry).
3. **Post a short teaser** on social with a single CTA: "Full experience at [your domain]."
4. **Reuse the same story** in newsletter, press email, and gig QR — same hub, different teasers.
5. **Measure on your site** (analytics in admin) — did people listen, return, and sign up?
6. **Later:** use news + social helpers to time announcements and reduce repetitive copy-paste work.

## Messaging angles that resonate with listeners

Frame the click as gain, not homework:

- "Hear it properly" (full player, lyrics, context)
- "Hear it first" (VIP / early access when tiers exist)
- "Hear what isn't on streaming" (site-exclusive tracks or versions)
- "Support us directly" (clear support link on your site)
- "One place for everything" (tour dates, news, music, visuals)

Avoid guilt-heavy "algorithm please notice us" energy. The listener chose you; give them a place worth staying.

## bandPromo's strategic position (for operators)

bandPromo is **self-hosted publishing software**. You run your installation. You are responsible for content, rights, hosting, and what you publish — see [OPERATOR-RESPONSIBILITY.md](OPERATOR-RESPONSIBILITY.md).

That independence is the marketing point:

- Your domain, your presentation, your audience relationship
- Coexist with Spotify, Bandcamp, and social — link out from **your** hub
- No central bandPromo catalogue competing with you for the same listener

The uphill battle against platform magnets is real. bandPromo's answer is not to outspend them — it is to make **your site** the place worth leaving the feed for, and to give you tools to say that clearly, repeatedly, and without rebuilding the same promo from scratch every release.

## Ambassadors, demos, and release handoffs

As bandPromo matures, experienced operators and beta testers can help **prospective operators** see a real site — not an empty shell.

**Release package export/import** (planned — see [PORTABILITY.md](PORTABILITY.md)) supports that workflow:

1. **Prepare on your install** — upload masters, embed artwork, write lyrics and descriptions (Markdown), assign living covers, complete release and EPK metadata.
2. **Export the release** — one ZIP with masters, linked visuals, and registry entries; human-readable names in the manifest only.
3. **Import on a demo or client install** — target operator gets a new release slot, runs Publish, and has a showcase-ready player.

**Why this matters for marketing**

- Ambassadors can demo bandPromo with **their own polished releases**, not generic placeholder content.
- Prospects see living covers, lyrics, and release storytelling immediately — the product sells itself on their domain.
- Skilled operators can offer **release preparation as a paid service** (metadata, artwork, living covers, EPK copy). bandPromo enables the portable handoff; pricing and contracts stay between you and your clients.

This is independent publishing extended to **people helping people** — without a central bandPromo marketplace or lock-in.
